# Vertex Lesson Review Checklist

## Scoring System

- **Pass**: The lesson is accurate, useful, complete, and ready for product use with only minor editorial changes.
- **Needs Revision**: The lesson has value but requires content, structure, accuracy, practice, or writing improvements before approval.
- **Reject**: The lesson is misleading, too shallow, poorly scoped, technically unsafe, or not aligned with Vertex learning standards.

## Learning Goals

- [ ] The lesson has one clear primary learning goal.
- [ ] The goal is appropriate for the learner's expected level.
- [ ] The goal is practical, not purely theoretical.
- [ ] The goal can be evaluated through learner output.
- [ ] The lesson does not try to teach too many concepts at once.
- [ ] The lesson outcome connects to real software engineering work.
- [ ] The lesson fits logically within its module.
- [ ] The lesson prepares the learner for the next lesson or task.

## Educational Quality

- [ ] The lesson explains the concept from first principles.
- [ ] The lesson teaches reasoning, not memorization.
- [ ] The lesson includes a useful mental model.
- [ ] The lesson reduces cognitive load through clear sequencing.
- [ ] The lesson moves from explanation to application.
- [ ] The lesson avoids unnecessary theory.
- [ ] The lesson supports beginner mistakes without oversimplifying.
- [ ] The lesson reinforces understanding through self-checking.

## Technical Accuracy

- [ ] All technical statements are correct.
- [ ] Terminology is used consistently.
- [ ] The lesson avoids outdated practices.
- [ ] The lesson avoids unsafe or misleading advice.
- [ ] The lesson distinguishes facts from recommendations.
- [ ] The lesson does not introduce advanced concepts too early.
- [ ] The lesson avoids hidden assumptions about prior knowledge.
- [ ] The lesson aligns with current professional practice.

## Examples

- [ ] Examples directly support the learning goal.
- [ ] Examples are small enough to understand quickly.
- [ ] Examples are realistic enough to feel useful.
- [ ] Examples avoid unnecessary setup.
- [ ] Examples use clear naming.
- [ ] Examples are technically correct.
- [ ] Examples avoid cleverness over clarity.
- [ ] Examples show the concept in action, not just syntax.

## Practice

- [ ] The lesson includes one focused practice task.
- [ ] The practice task requires applying the lesson concept.
- [ ] The task is small enough to complete in one sitting.
- [ ] The task has clear success criteria.
- [ ] The task avoids unrelated complexity.
- [ ] The task can be reviewed by a human or AI reviewer.
- [ ] The task encourages active problem solving.
- [ ] The task connects to employable engineering skills.

## Writing Quality

- [ ] The writing is clear and professional.
- [ ] The tone is direct and supportive.
- [ ] Sentences are concise.
- [ ] The lesson avoids textbook language.
- [ ] The lesson avoids unnecessary repetition.
- [ ] The lesson defines important terms when needed.
- [ ] The lesson explains why, not only what.
- [ ] The lesson is readable without external context.

## UX / Reading Experience

- [ ] The lesson has clear section headings.
- [ ] The lesson is easy to scan.
- [ ] Paragraphs are short enough for comfortable reading.
- [ ] Lists are used where they improve clarity.
- [ ] Code or structured material is visually separated.
- [ ] The lesson avoids long uninterrupted blocks of text.
- [ ] The reading flow feels natural from start to finish.
- [ ] The final transition prepares the learner for what comes next.

## Final Approval

- [ ] The lesson follows the official Vertex lesson template.
- [ ] The lesson matches the intended curriculum position.
- [ ] The lesson does not duplicate another lesson.
- [ ] The lesson does not introduce unsupported product promises.
- [ ] The lesson is ready for learner-facing use.
- [ ] The lesson can be reviewed consistently by another reviewer.
- [ ] The lesson supports Vertex's learn-build-review-improve loop.
- [ ] The final score is recorded as Pass, Needs Revision, or Reject.
